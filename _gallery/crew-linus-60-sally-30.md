<p>The composition takes its rule from Nathaniel Hellerstein’s Linus sequence, a binary sequence built one symbol at a time by comparing possible repetitions at its end. The companion Sally sequence records the length of the longer repetition avoided at each step. CREW translates these numerical constraints into a rhythm of coloured strips: apparent repetitions and changes arise from a rule rather than from a random arrangement. The catalogue connects this process to Linus’s attempt to break predictable patterns in a Peanuts true-or-false test. Paper, colour and spacing give a visible form to the tension between a fixed procedure and a complex result.</p>
