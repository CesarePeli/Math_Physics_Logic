# Dynamical Systems

**Dynamical systems** provide a mathematical framework for studying processes that evolve over time.

A system may be governed by precise deterministic laws and yet display remarkably complex behavior. Stability, periodicity, bifurcations, sensitivity to initial conditions, and chaos all emerge from the study of how states change under mathematical rules.

This field connects differential equations, geometry, topology, numerical analysis, and mathematical modelling.

## The Structure of the Lorenz Attractor

The **Lorenz attractor** is one of the most recognizable objects in the study of nonlinear dynamical systems and deterministic chaos.

Its characteristic geometry emerges from a simple system of differential equations, revealing how deterministic evolution can generate highly complex trajectories and extreme sensitivity to initial conditions.

The investigation focuses on the mathematical structure of the attractor and on the ideas that make the Lorenz system a fundamental example in modern dynamical systems theory.

## Order, Instability, and Chaos

Chaos does not mean the absence of mathematical structure. Chaotic systems are governed by deterministic laws, but their long-term evolution may depend so strongly on initial conditions that prediction becomes fundamentally difficult in practice.

Dynamical systems therefore offer a particularly rich meeting point between mathematical order and complex behavior.
