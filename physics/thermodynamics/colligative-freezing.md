# Freezing-Point Depression — Glucose in Water

<div class="content-box">

*By Prof. Marco Ruzzi.*

## Theoretical background

Pure deionized water freezes at 0 °C under the conditions considered here. A dissolved solute lowers its freezing point. For the glucose solution:

$$
|\Delta T|=K_{\mathrm{cr}}b.
$$

The cryoscopic constant K<sub>cr</sub> has units °C kg mol⁻¹. Molality is the amount of solute divided by the mass of **solvent** in kilograms:

$$
b=\frac{n_{\mathrm{solute}}}{m_{\mathrm{solvent}}}.
$$

</div>

## Exercise 8 — Freezing point of a glucose solution

What is the freezing point of a solution containing 25.0 g of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, in 250.0 g of water?

Use K<sub>cr</sub>=1.86 °C kg mol⁻¹ and a glucose molar mass of 180.157 g mol⁻¹.

### 1. Amount of glucose

$$
n_{\mathrm{glucose}}=\frac{25.0}{180.157}\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

$$
n_{\mathrm{glucose}}\simeq0.13877\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

### 2. Molality

The solvent mass is 250.0×10⁻³ kg:

$$
b=\frac{0.13877}{0.2500}\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}.
$$

$$
b\simeq0.5551\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}.
$$

### 3. Freezing-point depression

$$
|\Delta T|=(1.86)(0.5551)\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}.
$$

$$
|\Delta T|\simeq1.0325\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}.
$$

The freezing point is lowered by this amount, rather than raised:

$$
T_f\simeq-1.03\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}.
$$
